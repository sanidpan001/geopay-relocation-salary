Relocation Salary Calculator: What Your Pay Is Really Worth in Another City

A job offer of $120,000 in Austin is not the same thing as $120,000 in San Francisco. It sounds obvious, yet most people evaluate relocation offers by comparing the headline numbers — and then get an expensive surprise when rent alone eats the raise. A relocation salary calculator exists to fix exactly that mistake: it translates a salary from one city into the pay you would need in another city to keep the same standard of living, and tells you whether an offer is a genuine raise or a disguised pay cut.

GeoPay does this with a fully offline, baked-in dataset of more than 190 cities worldwide. There is no API call, no account, and no waiting — the numbers come from cost-of-living indices compiled from public knowledge, normalized so that New York City equals 100. Everything on this page is an estimate: good enough for planning, negotiating, and shortlisting cities, but never a substitute for local research or professional financial advice.

How an equivalent salary is calculated

The core idea behind any cost of living salary comparison is simple. Start with your current salary and subtract what life costs in your current city — housing, food, transport, and everything else. What remains is your disposable income: the money you can actually save, invest, or spend freely. Then ask: what salary in the target city would leave you with exactly the same disposable income?

GeoPay's formula is: equivalent salary = current salary + 12 × (monthly expenses in target city − monthly expenses in current city). If the target city is cheaper, the equivalent salary comes out lower than your current pay — meaning you could earn less on paper and still live just as well. If it is more expensive, the equivalent salary rises, and any offer below it is effectively a pay cut no matter how impressive the number looks.

This disposable-income method is more honest than the common shortcut of multiplying your salary by a cost-of-living ratio. Ratio multiplication assumes every dollar of your income scales with local prices, which is not true: savings, investments, and debt payments do not get cheaper when you move to a cheaper city. By anchoring on disposable income instead of raw ratios, GeoPay is an equivalent salary calculator moving cities onto one comparable scale: the money you actually keep.

Cost-of-living indices, explained honestly

GeoPay assigns every city a cost-of-living index with New York City at 100. A city indexed at 50 is estimated to cost roughly half as much for a comparable basket of goods and services; a city at 118, like Zurich, is estimated to cost about 18 percent more than New York. These indices are compiled from public knowledge of consumer-price baskets — groceries, restaurant meals, rent, utilities, transport, and everyday services — rounded into a single number.

A single number can never capture everything. Two people can live in the same city with wildly different budgets: a person who cooks at home and cycles to work spends far less than one who eats out daily and drives. GeoPay models a single professional with a modest lifestyle as its baseline. Families, luxury lifestyles, and ultra-frugal minimalists will all deviate from these estimates — in predictable directions, which is why the tool shows you the expense breakdown (housing, food, transport, other) so you can judge where your own spending differs.

Indices also move. Rents surge, currencies swing, and a dataset baked into a web page cannot track live markets. Treat the indices as a snapshot for comparison, and before signing anything, check current rents on local listings and current exchange rates from a live source.

Disposable income: the number that actually matters

Headline salary is a vanity metric. What determines your quality of life is salary minus unavoidable costs. GeoPay's dashboard puts disposable income after expenses front and center for both cities, side by side, so the comparison is visual and immediate. The verdict — real raise, pay cut, or roughly equal — is computed from the change in disposable income, not the change in salary.

Consider a concrete example. You earn the equivalent of $90,000 in Bangalore and receive a $110,000 offer in Seattle. The salary is 22 percent higher — but if Seattle's estimated living costs exceed Bangalore's by $25,000 a year, your disposable income actually falls, and GeoPay will label the move a pay cut. Flip the direction — $110,000 in Seattle to an $85,000 offer in Lisbon — and the lower number can still be a real raise if expenses drop by more than $25,000. This is the entire point of a relocation salary calculator: it stops you from being fooled by nominal figures.

Rent burden: the 30 percent rule

Housing is the single biggest lever in any move, which is why GeoPay includes a dedicated rent-burden dial. Rent burden is the share of your gross income spent on housing. The long-standing personal-finance guideline is to keep it under 30 percent: below that, the dial reads "Comfortable"; between 30 and 40 percent it reads "Stretched"; above 40 percent, "Overburdened".

Watch how the dial moves between your two cities. A move can raise your disposable income while simultaneously pushing your rent burden into the red — common when relocating from a city where you own a home to one where you will rent at market rates. Conversely, some offers look modest until you see the rent burden drop from 45 percent to 25 percent, which transforms daily life even if the salary barely changed. Landlords in many cities also apply their own version of this rule, often requiring income of three times the rent, so the dial doubles as a rough reality check on the apartments you will actually qualify for.

Savings rate: measuring your real raise

The second dial tracks your savings rate — disposable income as a share of salary. GeoPay rates it Excellent at 30 percent or more, Healthy between 15 and 30 percent, Tight between 0 and 15 percent, and Shortfall when expenses exceed income entirely. If your savings rate rises after a move, the offer is building your wealth faster regardless of what the headline salary says; if it falls, the move is costing you future freedom.

This framing is especially useful for early-career professionals. A 10 percent nominal raise that drops your savings rate from 25 percent to 10 percent is not a career win — it is lifestyle inflation imposed by geography. Negotiate with the savings-rate number in hand: "to keep my current savings rate, I would need X" is a far stronger argument than "I want more money," because it is anchored in arithmetic rather than appetite.

What this calculator deliberately leaves out

No free tool can model everything, and honest tools say so. GeoPay does not deduct income tax, because tax systems vary enormously — a move from Texas to California, or from Dubai to London, changes the tax picture more than the grocery bill does. It does not model healthcare costs, school fees, visa expenses, or the one-time cost of the move itself. Currency conversions use rough baked-in rates rather than live foreign-exchange data, so cross-currency comparisons carry extra uncertainty. And the expense basket assumes a single professional; add a partner, children, or a car, and your personal numbers will shift.

Use GeoPay as the first filter, not the final answer. If the tool says an offer is a 20 percent real raise, that is a strong signal worth pursuing with deeper research. If it says the offer is borderline, dig into the two or three expense categories that dominate your personal budget before deciding.

How to use the number in a negotiation

Employers relocating talent usually have a cost-of-living adjustment budget — but they rarely volunteer it. Walk in with three numbers from this page: the equivalent salary (your walk-away line for equal lifestyle), the rent burden at their offer (your quality-of-life argument), and the savings-rate change (your wealth argument). Ask for the equivalent salary plus a premium for the disruption of moving: new city, new network, new risks. Many relocation packages also include one-time help — moving costs, temporary housing, a signing bonus — which can bridge a gap that the base salary alone does not close.

If the employer cannot meet the equivalent salary, negotiate on the expense side instead: remote-work flexibility that lets you live in a cheaper suburb, housing assistance, or a cost-of-living review after twelve months. The dashboard gives you the vocabulary for all of these conversations.

Moving within a country vs. across borders

Domestic moves are the calculator's home turf: same currency, same tax system, and the cost-of-living index does most of the work. International moves add layers — exchange-rate risk, different tax regimes, visa constraints, and sometimes dramatically different definitions of a normal lifestyle. GeoPay handles the currency conversion with estimated rates and flags the result accordingly, but for international offers you should additionally research the destination's tax brackets, mandatory social charges, and healthcare system. A salary that looks generous pre-tax can be ordinary post-tax, and no cost-of-living index captures that.

Methodology and limitations, in brief

Each city's index estimates total consumer costs relative to New York City at 100, compiled from public knowledge of price baskets and rounded for stability. Monthly expenses scale linearly with the index from a New York baseline, then split into housing (40%), food (22%), transport (8%), and other (30%) — shares chosen to represent a modest single-professional lifestyle. Currency rates are rough estimates per US dollar. The verdict thresholds treat any disposable-income change within 1% of salary (or $500, whichever is larger) as "roughly equal" to avoid false precision. Nothing here is live data, nothing is personalized, and nothing is financial advice — it is a planning estimate, clearly labeled as such throughout the tool.

Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is this relocation salary calculator?

It is a planning estimate, not a precise quote. City cost indices are compiled from public knowledge and rounded, currency rates are rough baked-in estimates rather than live rates, and the expense basket models a single professional with a modest lifestyle. Use it to compare cities and anchor negotiations; before signing, verify current rents and exchange rates from live sources.

What is an equivalent salary when moving cities?

The equivalent salary is the pay you would need in the target city to end up with exactly the same disposable income — salary minus estimated living expenses — as you have now. GeoPay computes it as your current salary plus the annualized difference in estimated monthly expenses between the two cities. Any offer above it is a real raise in lifestyle terms; anything below is effectively a pay cut.

Why does the calculator ignore taxes?

Income tax varies enormously between cities, states, and countries — a move from Texas to California or from Dubai to London changes take-home pay more than groceries ever could. Modeling every tax regime in an offline tool would be misleading, so GeoPay compares gross salary against living costs and clearly labels the result an estimate. For a final decision, run the numbers through a tax calculator for both locations.

What is a good rent burden percentage?

The widely used guideline is to keep housing costs under 30% of gross income. GeoPay's rent-burden dial marks under 30% as Comfortable, 30–40% as Stretched, and over 40% as Overburdened. Many landlords also require income of roughly three times the rent, so the dial doubles as a check on which apartments you are likely to qualify for in the target city.

Should I accept a higher salary in a more expensive city?

Only if your disposable income — what is left after expenses — actually rises. Compare the offer against the equivalent salary GeoPay shows: if the offer beats it, your lifestyle improves; if not, the higher number is an illusion. Also check the rent-burden and savings-rate dials, since a move can raise disposable income while still making housing uncomfortably expensive.

Does it work for international moves and currency conversion?

Yes, with extra caution. The dataset covers 190+ cities across 60+ countries, and currency conversion uses rough baked-in exchange rates — not live forex data. International moves also involve different tax systems, visa costs, and healthcare models that the tool does not model, so treat cross-border results as a first filter and research the destination's tax and benefits system separately.
